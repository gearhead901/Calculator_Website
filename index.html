Calc Workshop

Engineering calculators for robotics builds. These are prototypes, and the newest version is first.

Prototype v13

Latest19 calculators

Shot Trajectory gets a shot map for software, like Team 4414’s: press a button to solve the most robust shot over a grid of distances and robot speeds toward or away from the goal, fit polynomials for launch angle, exit speed and flight time, and copy the Java code or download the table as CSV.

Prototype v12

19 calculators

Arm gets Step 3, the software handoff: pick a motion profile (Motion Magic or TrapezoidProfile, Gentle / Brisk / Race or custom), see the voltage and current the move needs, and get paste-ready feedforward gains, profile limits and a SingleJointedArmSim setup.

Prototype v11

19 calculators

The Arm calculator now works in two steps. Step 1 searches gear ratios (like a gear ratio planner) and recommends the fastest one that holds the arm within your holding-current target. Step 2 refines it with your real gear stages and their inertias.

Prototype v10

19 calculators

Adds an Arm calculator built on Mechanism Startup: gravity that changes with the arm angle, holding current, time to swing between two angles, and an inertia breakdown that includes the motor rotors and every gear-stage shaft.

Prototype v9

18 calculators

Backspin lift (Magnus effect) now works in both Shot Trajectory and Projectile, with separate on/off switches for air drag and lift. Both now use one shared, more accurate flight simulator and standard sea-level air density (1.225 kg/m³), so they always agree.

Prototype v8

18 calculators

Projectile adds a max fire rate: how fast you can shoot before a rising ball hits the one ahead of it near the top of the arc, with the balls drawn to scale on the trajectory. Covers shots that peak before the goal.

Prototype v7

18 calculators

Adds a Bolted Joint calculator in a new Fasteners group: bolt and member stiffness, preload and torque, yield, separation and fatigue safety factors (Shigley ch. 8), checked against the book's worked examples. Includes a thread stripping check (Machinery's Handbook): required thread engagement, which part fails first, and a good-practice engagement guide.
